Almost no farm starts from a blank sheet. There is already a program for the fields, one for the notebook, a portal for the region, a spreadsheet for the stock “because in season it is faster”, and the chat for warnings. Asking to switch all of it off in the name of a new archive is **the surest way to change nothing**. People go back to the tool they know on the day they are in a hurry. And hurry, on a farm, is the norm.

Centralising is not replacing. It is **stopping having five truths**.

## Why the programs stay, and why they are not enough

Each program was chosen for a reason. One prints the register in the shape the inspection expects. One holds the maps. One is the one the long-standing adviser can use with closed eyes. Throwing them away opens an operational hole before it creates order.

The limit is not that they exist. It is that **they do not pass the facts along**. The invoice enters the accounts and not the spray store. The map knows the cadastral hectare and not the buffer. The notebook is filled in a third place, copying from the other two. In a rush you go back to the spreadsheet, because the spreadsheet does not ask for ten clicks. Then the spreadsheet and the program diverge, and someone spends a morning pulling them back together.

Farms say it in concrete sentences. “We keep the software, but the production-rules notebook is still in a spreadsheet, otherwise we do not get there.” “The tractor map does not talk to the register, so we print.” It is not nostalgia. It is a missing bridge.

## Data is useful when it meets

The review of data analysis in agriculture shows how many sources already live side by side: surveys, images, histories. The bottleneck the authors describe is making them work for a decision, not piling up new ones. Keeping the program you have makes sense. **Keeping it as an island**, cut off from invoices and stock, reproduces that bottleneck in the form of copy and paste.
</ResearchNote>

A shared archive does not ask the farm to speak one language. It asks that the product name, the field and the quantity stay the same when they pass from one room to another.

## You start from what is already paper

The first bridge, the one that does not depend on agreements between software, is the document. A photographed invoice, a PDF, an exported sheet enter the archive, are read and suggested as lines. Whoever uses the usual program keeps using it for what it does well. They stop retyping the product list by hand.

Where a direct link with another program exists, it is an advantage: fewer exports, fewer files sent by email. It is not the requirement of day one. Seminai starts from documents and stock, and keeps operations as drafts until a person confirms them. The programs around can stay. What does not stay is **transcription as a job**.

## What not to promise on the first day

Do not promise that every regional portal, every tractor map and every cellar register will talk to each other by themselves within the week. Some links exist, others are still a file to export, others will not be there. Making that the condition for starting blocks the farm on what is missing.

The first day is smaller. You choose **a flow that today is all copying**: the invoices of products entering the store, for example. You photograph or upload them. You check the suggested lines. You see whether the stock that comes out resembles the shelf. If it does, you have removed a pour without switching anything off. If it does not, you have learned where the names do not match, which is still better than discovering it in December.

The other programs stay open. What changes is the direction of the copy. Instead of rewriting the fact in every room, you write it in the shared place and, when needed, you carry it into the program that has to print it or hand it in. It is **a habit, not a migration**. People keep it if the first flow saves a real hour, not if they are asked for faith in a future system.

The sign that the habit has held is ordinary. Mid-season, the new invoice is no longer asked for twice: once for the accounts and once for the notebook. The person in the field does not send the photo “to the adviser as well, so they can write it somewhere else”. The photo is already the draft line. The usual programs are still on. What has gone is the stretch done on foot, with a name retyped, between one and the other.

If mid-season the photo still travels twice, the shared place is not yet the real place. Someone does not trust it, or cannot find it. In that case you do not add a sixth program. You ask where the first invoice got stuck, and you fix that passage. A single archive is judged by **one less copy**, not by one more promise.
