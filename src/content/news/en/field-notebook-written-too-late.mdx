In many advisory offices the season’s papers arrive in waves. Some farms bring them in autumn, some in January, some only when an inspection is booked. The adviser opens envelopes, photos and sheets and tries to rebuild the sprays of April. The names are there, more or less. The dates are “that rainy week”. The quantities are guessed from what is left in the store.

The notebook that comes out of this work is a document. It is not the diary of the season. **It is a reconstruction**. It can look compliant on paper and sit far from what happened between the rows.

## Why it gets postponed

During a spray nobody wants to open a program. Hands are busy, the light changes, the weather sets the pace. A scrap note in the cab, or a message at the end of the row, is as much as the real work allows. The office comes later.

Later slips. There are many farms. Complete papers arrive from only some of them. The rest is chased by phone. Filling everything at year end looks efficient: one push, with the person who “knows how the notebook is written”. In fact it squeezes **ten months of work** into a few days, with less information than existed on the day of the spray.

There is another habit: the notebook as a clerical task, detached from the person who decides in the field. The person spraying and the person writing are not the same. If they do not talk while the season runs, **in December one of them has to guess**.

## What no longer fits

Doses get adjusted so the stock will close, not so the tank is described. Fields are chosen because “we usually do it there”. A withdrawn product or a harvest interval is missed, because nobody looks at the moment when the plan could still change.

Offices that reread other people’s registers find mistakes almost every time. Not from bad faith. From accumulation. A figure copied wrong in March, repeated on the following lines, is the truth of the file by December. Fixing it means **rewriting the story**, not changing one cell.

European rules on the records professional users keep for plant protection products are moving toward an electronic format, with a clear minimum: **product, date, quantity, area, crop**. That is Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/564. It is not a style tip. It is the list of what someone may later ask for. Rebuilding it from memory is possible. It is also the most fragile way to be ready.

## A tool used only at the finish line stays outside the work

A British study of farmers and advisers found fifteen reasons why a decision tool is actually used. Among them are habit, the effort required, and whether the tool also serves legal paperwork, not only an agronomic tip. If filling the register is extra work, detached from field days, it stays **a year-end task**. By then much of the information is already gone.
</ResearchNote>

The study is not about the Italian notebook. It says something farms recognise: people use **what sits inside the day**, not what is opened in December to close a file.

## A register that grows with the season

The useful notebook is the one that thickens when the work happens. The note of the day, the invoice of the week and the stock stay in the same archive. At year end you read and confirm. You do not reconstruct.

## What to keep from the day, even in ten seconds

You do not need a perfect sentence. You need **four pieces**, written when you step off the tractor or the same evening: the field, the product, a quantity even if rough, the day. The rest (the exact trade name, the pack conversion, the buffer taken off the area) is completed in the office while memory is fresh, not in January.

An office that follows many farms can ask only for this, and can refuse the rest of the reconstruction. An envelope at year end saying “I marked the sprays on the calendar” is not a late notebook. It is a missing notebook. You see the difference from the date of the notes, not from the date someone typed them.

There is a simple way to notice that you are reconstructing. If to fill a line you must ask “was it before or after the rain?”, **the line is already an estimate**. Estimates can be declared as such. They become a problem when, copied into the register, they look like a measured fact. An archive that separates the note of the day from the confirmed line keeps that difference visible.

An electronic register does not cure the habit by itself. If you fill it in December with the same memories, it is only a stiffer form. The gain appears if during the season the four pieces are already in the archive, and **in December you read the exceptions**: the note without a product, the product without an invoice, the field whose area does not fit.
