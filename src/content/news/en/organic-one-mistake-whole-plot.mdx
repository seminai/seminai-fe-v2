People who follow organic farms say it plainly: an ordinary notebook, filled to close the season, does not by itself prove that organic rules were kept. More lists are needed. Operations. Inputs that came in. Sales that went out. And above all **the link to the plot**, not to “the vineyard” said in general.

A product that is not allowed, used by mistake or written on the wrong field, is not a line you erase. The plot concerned **may have to start conversion again**. That is years, not an afternoon in the office.

## What the rules ask, in practice

Regulation (EU) 2018/848 is the basic text of European organic production. On the point that matters in the field, the annex is direct: if a plot is treated with a product or substance not authorised for organic production, the authority requires **a new conversion period** for the plots concerned. The period is the ordinary one: at least two years before sowing for many crops, at least three years before the first harvest for perennial crops such as vines. There are narrow exceptions, for example a treatment imposed by the authority. There is no exception for “we noticed later and tidied the sheet”.

Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/1691 adds how you write it down. For products used in the field, records keep the date, the name, the quantity, the crop and the plots. **A farm total is not enough.** Two neighbouring vineyards can have different histories. The register has to know that.

Then there is copper. Copper compounds allowed as plant protection products may be used only in applications that, added up, do not exceed **28 kilograms of copper per hectare** over seven years. That ceiling is set by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/1981. Seven years is longer than a season and longer than the memory of a sheet rewritten every March. Without a history attached to the plot, the ceiling is discovered when it has already been passed.

This is not a research paper. It is the European frame for organic production and for copper use. It asks for records by plot and, if a product that is not allowed lands on a field, a new conversion period for that field. Copper has a multi-year ceiling. Respecting it needs the history of previous years, not only today’s spray.

## Where scattered data goes wrong

The plot is wrong. The note says “the new vineyard”, and the farm has two new vineyards, only one of them organic. The line lands on the other.

The product is wrong. A similar trade name, a loan from the neighbour, an old non-organic pack left on the same shelf. If warehouse and register cannot see each other, the mistake enters the notebook and leaves only at inspection.

Copper is wrong because each year has a different sheet, or because the area used in the sum is not the area sprayed. A few extra kilograms, repeated, pass the ceiling without anyone meaning to.

People who advise organic farms end up watching the typing line by line. Not from distrust of people. Because the consequences of one line are long, and the starting documents are fragmented.

## The organic shelf is not the shelf of everything else

Many mistakes are not born in the field. They are born in one warehouse, where an allowed pack and a forbidden pack sit a hand’s width apart, with similar names. The person takes the one within reach. The notebook, filled later, inherits the choice or corrects it on paper without correcting what was applied. The two versions are worse than one: the sheet is clean, the land is not.

Separating, in the archive, what may enter an organic plot from what may not, is a warehouse act before it is a register act. When a spray is suggested on an organic field, **a product off the list shows up at once**. Not because the program “knows farming” in the abstract. Because that field is marked organic and that product is not among the inputs allowed for that field.

Copper asks for the same continuity. Each confirmed use adds copper metal, not “a pass” or “a bottle”. The plot card shows the running total of the years the ceiling considers, not only the current season. The person deciding the next spray **sees whether there is room, before filling the tank**. It is an adviser’s calculation. The archive saves them from redoing it on five sheets that do not share the same area.

Once a year it is worth rereading **the card of every organic plot**: confirmed products, cumulative copper, the area used in the sum. It is not the notebook you hand in. It is the internal check that says whether the archive and the farm’s memory still agree. If that reading shows a name nobody remembers applying, there is still time to see whether it is a writing error or a field error. Waiting for the inspection to do this reading is how a detail becomes a conversion period.

The warning is not a verdict. The person who knows the farm can explain a similar name or a boundary. They need to see it when they can still choose not to spray, or when they can still correct the line before it becomes the official version.
