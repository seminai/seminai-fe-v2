It is January 2027. On the office shelf sits the paper record of the season just closed: pencil lines, a few corrections, a page with a coffee stain. Until yesterday it was enough to keep it tidy and store it. With **digital spray records**, from 2027 that is no longer enough: those lines had to become a file by 31 December 2026.

In Italy this record is the *quaderno di campagna*, the mandatory register of plant protection treatments. The change does not come out of nowhere. It comes from a 2023 EU regulation, postponed once, and from an Italian decree published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale in April 2026. In between there are plenty of rumours, some of them wrong, and one piece still missing: AGEA's instructions on how the record will connect to the farm file.

This guide sets out what the rules say, what is still open and what is worth doing in the weeks left. Every step points to the original document, listed at the end.

## Where the duty to keep a record comes from

The treatment register is not new with digital. **The duty has existed since 2009** and is written in Article 67 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, the EU text on plant protection products. Professional users must keep, for at least three years, a record with the product name, the time and dose of application, and the area and crop treated. On request, the record must be shown to the authority.

In Italy the rule arrived in Legislative Decree No 150 of 2012, Article 16. That is where the name everyone uses in the field comes from, *registro dei trattamenti* (treatment register), and that is where the practical details are.

### What Italian law asks today

Decree 150 asks for each treatment to be entered **by the harvest and in any case within thirty days** of the treatment. Each line needs the farm's details, the crop and its extent in hectares, the date, the product, the quantity in kilos or litres and the pest or disease that made the treatment necessary.

The register is kept for the three years after the year of the treatments. Someone other than the holder may fill it in: in that case the holder signs it at the end of the year. Farms can rely on a CAA, the agricultural assistance centre to which many farms entrust their paperwork.

Until now none of these rules said how to write it. Paper, spreadsheet, software: anything went, as long as the content was there.

## What changes with EU Regulation 2023/564

In March 2023 the European Commission published Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/564. It does not change what is recorded, it changes **how it is recorded**. It applies from 1 January 2026.

The core point is one: the record must be in an electronic, machine readable format. Each use must be entered without undue delay. If the entry starts in another form, for example in the paper notebook, it must be converted into the electronic format within 30 days of use, unless a Member State sets shorter periods.

### What machine readable means

The definition comes from a 2019 EU directive on open data: a file structured so that software can easily identify and extract specific data. In practice, **a photo of the notebook is not enough**, and neither is a scanned PDF. The date, dose or crop of a line must be separate fields that software can read without someone copying them out.

### The minimum lines for a field treatment

The regulation's annex lists what each line must contain. For treatments on surfaces, such as fields and non permanent greenhouses:

- the product name and its authorisation number;
- the date and, where relevant, the start time;
- the dose in kilos or litres per hectare;
- the plot, identified by the land unit of the CAP geospatial system or by another method set by the Member State;
- the hectares treated;
- the crop, with its name, and where relevant the growth stage.

For crop and stage the regulation asks Member States to provide **names linked to common codes**: EPPO codes, an international classification of plants, and the BBCH scale, which numbers growth stages (bud burst, flowering and so on). They are the same names for everyone, so two different records can be compared.

There are separate lists for treatments in stores and permanent greenhouses and for seed treatment.

## Why the Italian deadline is 31 December 2026

In 2025 several Member States reported needing more time to train users, in particular small and older farmers. The Commission responded with Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2203, published on 3 November 2025. **It postpones nothing by itself**: it lets Member States waive conversion for uses before 1 January 2027.

Italy decided with the decree of the Ministry of Agriculture of 30 December 2025, adopted in agreement with the Regions and published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale general series No 94 of 23 April 2026. Article 1 is short. Records of uses on Italian territory **must be converted into the electronic format by 31 December 2026**.

So in 2026 you can still write on paper, but the year's data must be brought into digital form by the end of December. The Emilia-Romagna Region's integrated production bulletin of 20 March 2026 put it this way: for 2026, joining is still voluntary. The decree does not use the word voluntary. It only sets the deadline.

The date of 31 January 2027 is also circulating for 2026 data. **It is not in the decree**, and we did not find it in any official document. The date to keep in mind is 31 December 2026.

### And from 1 January 2027?

The decree sets no deadlines for later years. From 2027 the EU rules therefore apply: record without undue delay and, if starting from paper, convert within 30 days of use. The timing of Decree 150, which asks for entries within thirty days, also remains. In short, **from 2027 the digital line has to be written within a month**, not at the end of the season.

## The record and the farm file

The farm file, *fascicolo aziendale*, is the farm's official folder on SIAN, the national agricultural information system run by AGEA. It holds land, crops and CAP applications. It is usually updated by the CAA.

Since the 2024 campaign AGEA has included in the farm file the QDCA, the digital version of the farmer's field record. Operating instructions No 58 of 20 May 2024 describe it as a voluntary tool. It can be filled in on the SIAN portal or from the CAA's software. Those who use it get **a lower risk rating in control sampling** and checks that can be done remotely.

In January 2026 another EU regulation arrived, 2026/148. It removed a requirement from Regulation (EU) 2022/1173: information on plant protection use inside the CAP geospatial aid application, for certain interventions linked to reducing pesticides. The reason is simplification: farmers already keep the Article 67 record, so the same data need not be reported twice.

It should be read with care. **The regulation does not forbid linking the record to the farm file**: it removes a requirement, not a tool. How it will work in practice depends on AGEA. The farm file instructions for 2026 said the QDCA rules would come in a later act. As of 12 November 2026 none has been published, and the farm file instructions for the 2027 campaign, issued on 11 November, do not mention it. We will update this guide when they arrive.

## What to do in the weeks left

31 December is close, and December on a farm is already full. The work splits in two: closing 2026 and preparing 2027.

### Closing 2026

The first step is knowing where this year's record really is. Often it is not in one place: a notebook in the office, notes from whoever sprayed, product invoices, a few messages. Before converting, the lines have to be gathered and checked for all fields, **especially the area treated and the dose per hectare**, which are often missing on paper.

Then there is the choice of tool. The QDCA on SIAN, the CAA's software, a farm management program: the rules ask for a format, not a product. What matters is that the file can be read by software and contains the fields in the annex. If the CAA or an adviser keeps the record, the question to ask today is simple: **who converts 2026, and with which tool**.

### Preparing 2027

From 1 January the digital line has to be written within a month of the treatment. It is worth deciding now who writes it, and when. One hour a week costs less than a week at the end of the season, and the data are more accurate.

It is also worth aligning names. Plots named as in the farm file, crops with their official name, products with their authorisation number: these are the fields the regulation asks for, and the ones on which an inspection compares the record with the rest of the documents.
