A four-page invoice, crooked, photographed against the light. Production rules of twenty pages. A label with a dose per hectare and a string of limits. Nobody wants to reread all of this every Monday for every farm. It is comparison work, repetitive, where the eye tires and a digit jumps.

A program can do the first pass. It reads. It matches the product name to the one in the archive. It compares the quantity with the field area and with what is in the store. It highlights **the line that does not fit**: a date before the purchase, a dose above the maximum, a product that does not belong in organic farming.

Then it stops. The line is not yet the register.

## Why the signature is not delegated

The person who signs a notebook, a plan or a piece of advice answers for that text toward the farm, toward the inspection, toward the customer. A program is not in the field and does not see the excluded row, the ditch, the drum lent between neighbours. It sees the documents it was given. If those documents are incomplete, the suggestion is incomplete. Letting it pass as final **turns a help into a risk**.

There is also a craft reason. Experienced advisers trust a tool when they can see what it rests on. A number without a label, without a field and without stock is an opaque number. It is accepted in a hurry, and then nobody looks at it. Human checking is not a romantic brake. It is how **the suggestion stays usable over time**.

The European regulation on artificial intelligence (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) separates systems by risk and, for the more sensitive uses, expects a person to be able to oversee them. This page is not legal advice and does not say which box your case falls into. The practical sense, on a farm, is narrower: **the draft does not replace the person who knows the field**.

In the interviews of that study, advisers trusted a tool if they could see where the answer came from and if the base was solid. If they could not see the reasoning, the tool stayed on the shelf. An automatic suggestion of a dose or of a register line works the same way: **it has to be accompanied by the document, the field and the rule**, or the person who must sign has no reason to believe it.
</ResearchNote>

## How the work goes, in practice

The draft is visible as a draft. You can see what was read and what is uncertain. Correcting a name or a quantity is a short gesture, not a retype of the whole invoice. What is accepted **stays distinct from what was only suggested**. In an inspection you can say: this line was confirmed by this person, on this date, starting from this document.

It is the same principle as the message from the field. The voice note warns. The official line is born afterwards. Guidelines on how people work with an automatic system ask for exactly this easy correction, before the final text. We talk about that in [WhatsApp is handy, but it is not a register](/en/news/whatsapp-is-not-a-register/).

## What a suggestion must show if it is to be checked

Four things, **visible without hunting in another program**. The document the line was born from, so the invoice or the note can be reopened. The field and the area used for the calculation, so you can see whether the whole map or the sprayed part was taken. The rule or the label the dose was compared with, in a sentence, not in a code. The status: draft, or confirmed by a person on a date.

If one of these four is missing, the suggestion is an opaque number. It can still be accepted, from tiredness. It cannot be defended, and it cannot be taught to someone new in the office. If all four are there, the check is short precisely because it is possible. You look at the difference, you do not redo the work.

This is also how a farm avoids two extremes. The first is to trust and nothing else, and to find the mistake at the inspection. The second is never to trust, and to keep typing everything by hand “to be safe”, throwing away the time the program was meant to return. The middle way is **a draft that lets itself be corrected** and that remembers it was corrected.

In an office the test is concrete. Take an invoice already read and ask two people, one experienced and one new, whether the suggested line is acceptable. If both find the document, the field and the reason for the flag without help, the suggestion can be checked. If only the person who built the system knows why that number came out, the suggestion is not ready for the register. The criterion is not how “intelligent” the pass is. It is whether **a person can say no in a minute**, with the evidence in front of them.

Seminai is drawn this way: it reads documents, suggests doses and flags anomalies against labels and rules, and leaves operations unconfirmed until a person approves them. The saving is in the preparation. The judgment stays with the person who signs.

That judgment, on the dose, needs the right area. Without it, even a careful suggestion starts from an inflated number: [The right dose is calculated on the area actually sprayed](/en/news/right-dose-real-area/).
