Production rules and certification standards describe obligations within a defined scope. A useful digital record must preserve the source and its context rather than turning a PDF into an unexplained instruction.

## Identify the document

Record the publishing body, crop or activity, region, version and validity information. A regional production rule, certification standard and internal method are different kinds of material and should remain distinguishable.

A document from a previous campaign should not silently stand in for the current one. Verify the applicable source with a competent professional.

## Connect a rule to the right context

Knowing a product name appears in a document is not enough to determine whether a proposed treatment is appropriate. The farm, crop, period and actual source restrictions matter. A purchase invoice alone does not supply those facts.

## Publish only what can be shared

A public catalogue should contain information and source links the publisher is authorised to disclose. Remove farm-specific notes and confidential annotations before publication. Public availability does not itself certify that a rule has been reviewed or applies to another farm.

## Keep responsibility visible

Use the catalogue to find a source and understand its version. Professional review is still needed before it informs a real operation. A usage counter is not evidence of correctness or current legal validity.

Review an entry when its source or validity changes. A digital copy has value when people can tell which source it represents and what still needs checking.
