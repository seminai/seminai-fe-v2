Agronomy practices often organise the same public source documents separately. Sharing a well-described source can reduce repeated preparation, provided the publication preserves context and does not expose farm-specific information.

## What belongs in a public entry

A useful entry identifies the type of rule, relevant region, version and original source. Its description should explain the scope rather than promise that the rule applies to every reader.

Distinguish a production rule from a certification standard, an internal method or a general best practice. Those categories carry different responsibilities.

## Check before publishing

Confirm that the metadata and linked material can be shared. Remove personal information, client notes and negotiated settings. Review any attachment or annotation; a public flag is not a substitute for checking the material.

## Reuse with context

Finding a public rule does not automatically assign it to a farm or make it suitable for a treatment. The professional must check source, version, period and relevance. Where an application supports copying a rule, a private draft still needs review before operational use.

## Keep sources current

A publication should be revisited when its source or validity changes. Visits and adoption counts can help people discover an entry, but do not demonstrate technical review or legal validity.
