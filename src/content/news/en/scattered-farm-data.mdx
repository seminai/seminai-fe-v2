On a medium-sized wine farm the same week produces a supplier invoice, a photo of a delivery note, a file from the regional portal, a map of the plots and a sheet where the adviser writes the sprays. Each object holds a true piece. **None holds the whole week**.

Whoever must answer a practical question (“did this product come in, was it used, on which vineyard?”) does the round. Email, then spreadsheet, then farm software, then a call to the person who was in the field. If people are in a hurry, the answer is “more or less”. An inspection **does not accept “more or less”**.

## It is not mess. It is work split into rooms

The programs farms use were born for one job. One keeps the accounts. One draws the fields. One is for filling the notebook when the season is already over. A regional portal wants its own files. The phone holds the messages of the person on the tractor.

Between one room and the next there is always **a person copying**. They copy the product name, the quantity, the date, the plot number. Every copy is a chance to miss a letter, lose a batch, forget a discount, or write the whole area instead of the area sprayed.

Offices that follow dozens of farms see this round every day. Documents arrive in different shapes. Some delivery notes describe the product so vaguely that someone has to phone the supplier to learn the pack and the quantity. The hard part is not “going digital”. It is **rebuilding a fact that already happened**, but was written somewhere the others cannot see.

## What gets lost

The thread of time gets lost. A March invoice and an April spray look like two stories, until someone puts them on the same line and finds that the product used was not in the store yet, or that it is still there because the spray was never written down.

The right name gets lost. The same product appears as a trade name on the invoice, as a nickname in the message, and as an active substance in the production rules. If the three texts never meet, the register and the warehouse talk about different things while pointing at the same can.

Trust in the number gets lost. When the spreadsheet stock does not match the boxes on the shelf, the temptation is to **edit a date or a quantity** until the totals close. The sheet looks square. The story of the field does not.

## The research, in plain words

A 2017 review looks at how farms produce many kinds of information: images, sensors, histories of work. The step that stays hard, even where data already exists, is **using it for a decision**. Different sources do not read themselves. They need order and a point where they meet. Without that point, collecting more material does not make the farm clearer.
</ResearchNote>

You do not need a satellite. The review describes a problem that also shows up with PDFs and sheets: the pieces exist, and **the decision waits for someone to stitch them**.

## One archive, many doors

The single place is not a bigger desk. It is an archive where the invoice, the field and the stock movement are the same object seen from different sides. The photo of a document comes in through one door. The field note comes in through another. The product name is recognised once and then reused.

Seminai does this with documents farms already have: invoices, delivery notes, notes. Extraction suggests the lines. A person checks them. From there the warehouse and the register do not start from zero. The programs you use for other jobs can stay. The archive does not ask you to rebuild the farm. It asks you to **stop retyping**.

## A test for one week

Pick a product bought this month. Follow only that. Where is the invoice. Where is it written in the stock. If someone already used it, where is the note. If it reached the notebook, under which name. Time **how long it takes to answer** without a phone call.

If the answer comes in a few minutes, that corner of the farm is already an archive, even if it lives on a careful sheet. If you must open email, a drawer and a program, and the name changes between them, the data is scattered. It does not matter that each file looks tidy. The thread is broken.

Repeat the test on a field, not only on a product. Take one plot. What area is on the cadastral sheet, what area was used for the last spray, what area the driver has in mind. Three different numbers are normal. The problem is not knowing which of the three is in charge when you buy and when you record.

The list that comes out is short. It is usually not “the whole farm”. It is product invoices, the list of fields with the area you really use, and the stock. The rest can wait. What cannot wait is to **stop rewriting these three** every week.
