A dose proposal needs more than arithmetic. A competent person must establish the product, crop, actual treated area, applicable sources and relevant history. An AI assistant can help gather those elements without becoming the person responsible for the decision.

## What a useful workflow shows

Start with a question such as: which facts and sources need checking before proposing a treatment for this plot? This describes a support workflow, not advice to use a specific product.

The assistant should identify the relevant source, distinguish the farm and plot context, state missing information and make any calculation inspectable. If a label or applicable rule is unavailable, the answer should expose that limitation.

## Sources do not become guarantees

An assigned document may be outdated, incomplete or inapplicable to the crop or period. A catalogue entry does not establish permission to use a product. The reviewer needs the source version and its scope, not just a plausible sentence from an assistant.

The same applies to farm history: a missing record must not be treated as proof that no previous treatment occurred.

## Keep a proposal separate from a confirmed record

A suggested dose or operation is a draft. The professional checks source, context and calculation before confirming the entry. Corrections should remain understandable to whoever reviews the record later.

## Questions to bring to a demo

Ask which sources are actually available, how conflicting documents are handled, which missing facts stop a proposal and how corrections reach the final record. Use a defined demonstration case instead of assuming every crop and regulation is covered.
