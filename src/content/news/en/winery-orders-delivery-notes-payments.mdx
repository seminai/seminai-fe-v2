A winery prepares an order for a restaurant. The customer is in an address book. The bottles leave a shelf counted on a sheet. The delivery note is written by hand, or in a program that never saw the order. The courier is told with a message. The payment is checked the following week, on another statement. If something does not fit (a case short, a transfer late) the day is rebuilt backwards.

It is the same mechanism as the field notebook, moved a few metres, from the vineyard to the barrel room. One fact. Many files. **One person acting as the bridge**.

## What gets rewritten

The customer is rewritten: name, address, tax number, on every document. The bottles are rewritten: format, vintage, count. The price is rewritten, with the risk that the order and the delivery note do not state the same figure. The instruction for the person shipping is rewritten out loud.

None of these copies is hard. **In a week of shipments they become the work**. And they leave typical holes. The delivery note exists, the order does not, and nobody remembers what was promised. The payment exists, the delivery note belongs to another customer with a similar name. The stock still shows bottles that are already on the van, because the exit was written only on the delivery note.

Medium wineries know this as inertia: “we have always done it this way, and with today’s volumes we still can”. The “we still can” is time of the same people who should be with the vines and the cellar. Going digital, for many of them, is not the wish for a new program. It is **the tiredness of rewriting**.

## A chain is a chain of marks

<ResearchNote
  label="What the research says"
  source="Aung and Chang, 2014. Traceability in a food supply chain. Food Control."
  href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodcont.2013.11.007"
>
The traceability review describes the ability to follow a food from one step to the next, in production and in distribution. Each step has to leave a mark linked to the previous one. For a winery the commercial steps are the order, the exit from stock, the delivery note and the payment. If one of these lives in a file the others cannot see, **the chain breaks at the most ordinary point**: the sale.
</ResearchNote>

You do not need a code on every bottle to see the principle. You need the case that left to be the same case as the order and the same case as the document. The mark can be **a shared line, not a new stamp**.

## One thread, without promising the whole accounts

The realistic step is to stop treating the order, the document and the stock as texts to retype. When a purchase or sales document enters an archive, **the bottles and the products move with it**. The person checking the payment starts from that line, not from a detached sheet.

## Follow one shipment all the way

Try it with an order from a week ago. Find who asked for what. Find what left the shelf. Find the document it left with. Find whether the payment arrived. If to do that you open four places and ask two people, the story is split even if each piece, alone, is correct.

The minimum to keep together is modest: customer, lines, exit date, document. The payment can stay in the bank statement. What is almost always missing is not treasury software. It is **the document number written next to the order**, so the person checking the statement knows which shipment they are looking at. Without that bridge, “a transfer is missing” is a hunt. With that bridge, it is a line.

Wineries that already have a cellar register can leave it where it is. The hole is at the edge of the register, where the wine ends and the customer begins. It is the same edge that, in the vineyard, sits between the product invoice and the notebook. Treating it as a problem of “wine software” makes it bigger than it is. It is a problem of not rewriting.

The change shows on a week of shipments, not on a project. Count how many times the customer’s name is retyped and how many times someone phones to ask whether the case has left. If those calls fall because the exit line is the same as the order, the thread is there. The courier, the price list and the bank can remain separate tools. What must not stay separate is **the identity of the shipment**: the same case, the same document, the same customer.

The person packing the shipment and the person checking the payment should not have to retell the day out loud. One shared line, with the document number, is enough for “it left on Tuesday” and “the transfer is not there” to talk about the same case. The rest of the winery (ageing, the cellar book, visits) continues with the tools it already has. The commercial thread stops being a person who remembers.
